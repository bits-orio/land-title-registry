# The cell chronicle is a per-cell speedrun leaderboard, not a first-come log

Every (planet, cell) keeps a chronicle: the teams that have deeded that cell, ranked by **how fast** they did it on **their own team clock** — not by who got there first in wall time. The top three render as tiny gold/silver/bronze text lines under the cell's top edge (centered) on every surface of that planet, so each team sees the standings on its own copy. This ships the v2 "ghost borders" idea, sharpened, pulled into v1 by the designer during playtesting.

Two semantics matter and both came from the designer directly:

- **Fastest, not first.** A speedrunning team that starts later but deeds cell (x, y) in less team-clock time *rightfully displaces* earlier entries, and the drawn rankings re-sort to match. (This also reframes the old "newer records overshadow older ones" rejection: that was about *points* being keyed to unstable records — the chronicle is celebration and standings only, no points, so displacement is the feature, not the instability.)
- **Team clocks, absolute fallback.** Times are measured from each team's own clock (`mts-v1` `get_team_info().clock_start_tick` — the getter already existed; zero MTS changes), which keeps MTS staged starts fair. Without MTS, or for non-team forces, times are absolute game time.

Mechanics:

- **Only competing teams are ranked.** Under MTS the `player` force (pre-join) and `spectator` force are not contenders — `get_team_info` returning nil is the signal — and they receive no starter Deed either. Without MTS every player force competes. Play surfaced both failure modes as ranked "Team player" and "Team spectator" entries at 0:00.
- A team's time is set by its **first** Deed of the cell; re-deeding later never improves it. Entries are achievements: they are keyed by planet (not surface), survive downgrades and surface clears, and merge best-time-wins on force merges.
- **Only two outcomes are celebrated** (playtest call): being **first** to Deed a cell, and **taking** its fastest record from someone. Placing 2nd or 3rd is not an achievement — it lands in the standings and nowhere else. Recognition is an MTS-style on-screen banner (zoom-stable `draw_text` above each force member, expiring via `time_to_live` so the no-idle-tick discipline holds), plus a **single** chat line per survey-tool action carrying team, actor, cells, cost, and a record clause only when a record was set. An earlier design printed two chat lines per unlock and celebrated every placement; both were noise. `ltr-show-celebrations` (per-player) mutes the banner.
- The earlier per-cell-contest gate (require 2+ entries before any recognition) is gone: the first team to reach a cell now gets its own moment, which is fairer and removes the "nothing happens on a fresh map" dead zone. The unit of competition is the **force**, not the player: two players sharing a force are one competitor, so celebrations are inherently an MTS-shaped (or otherwise multi-force) feature, and single-force play keeps the quiet personal log without "Fastest!" spam on every deed. `/ltr-debug` states which of these conditions is unmet on the cell you are standing on.
- Standings are drawn in the **team's own color with its leader in brackets**, via `mts-v1`'s `get_team_label` — a team reads identically everywhere Land Title Registry names it. No rank-specific palette exists (an earlier gold/silver/bronze scheme was removed for exactly this reason). Labels are re-fetched rather than stored so renames and leader changes stay live, memoized within a tick so a full rebuild costs one call per force.
- **Chat scales down, the map scales up.** With a dozen teams a contested cell changes hands about H(n) times, so server-wide record broadcasts turn the race into spam; records are told to the two teams involved only, and per-claim lines are off by default (`ltr-print-claims`). The standings themselves live on the map, where they cost nothing to read and nobody has to be interrupted.
- **Chronicle text is O(developed area), unlike frontier-only borders.** Accepted: it is the feature. The map-view family mirrors the world one (coordinate plus top three) because chart text is world-anchored — it shrinks with zoom, so a full block scales with the cell and can never bleed into its neighbours. An intermediate design showed only the record holder in map view to halve the object count; playtesting showed the room was there, and legibility won.
- Rendering follows the derived-state discipline: chronicle text objects rebuild through the same reconcile path as blockers and borders (`/ltr-rebuild`, surface sweeps, re-enables), with their own bookkeeping so frontier refreshes never clobber them.
- Display names resolve at draw time through the `mts-v1` provider, so team renames stay current.
